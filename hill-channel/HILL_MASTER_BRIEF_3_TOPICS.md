## 2. Writing rules (all 3 scripts)

**Style**
1. Write pure motivational mentor talk, like the old Napoleon Hill hits: talk to "you", be commanding, ask questions, give steps to do ("do this tonight"). It must NOT read like a book.
2. **NEW FRAME (owner 2026-11-04):** the lesson comes from **Napoleon Hill's life and writing**: "We learned this from Hill's life. In such-and-such year, on such-and-such occasion, he said/wrote this." Full rules are in section 3. The old book (Conwell/Black/Hunter) is now OPTIONAL: at most one line of support.
3. NEVER speak as the author or Hill in first person ("For 25 years I studied…" is forbidden).

**Length and shape**
4. About 9,800 words (~55–60 min), in about 12 sections. Each section gets a label the ear can hear ("Step two.", "The second friend.", etc.).
5. Each section follows this pattern: the viewer's situation in one sentence → the idea → a plain explanation → one instruction they can do today.
6. At every section boundary, re-hook with a question or a withheld detail. Never recap ("so far we have seen…").

**Hook (first 30 seconds)**
7. Sentence one has "you" and a real moment from the viewer's day (alarm, car, inbox, phone, mirror). No author, no year, no "we".
8. Use one of these shapes: question volley / contradiction ("You think X. It isn't.") / pain recognition.
9. In the first 30 s, plant an open loop (a sentence, a test, or one rule that you will give later). Pay it off at 55–65% of the runtime.
10. No table of contents ("here's what you'll learn…" is forbidden).

**Sentences (TTS)**
11. Average 8–11 words per sentence, hard cap 22. One idea per sentence.
12. **Zero digits.** Write every number and year in words ("nineteen fourteen").
13. Use 6–8 "you/your" per 100 words, and 8–15 questions per 1000 words, asked in volleys.
14. One anaphora triplet per section ("Before you… Before you… Before you…"). Don't overdo it.
15. Write 3–6 sentences per paragraph. No [pause] or tags.

**Quotes and honesty (YPP)**
16. Use only 1–3 quotes, verbatim and copied exactly from the source text, each 25 words or fewer, with attribution. Never put paraphrase inside quotation marks.
17. NEVER: invent a quote, story, dialogue or number; threaten the viewer; promise a guaranteed result; give doctor, money or legal advice.
18. Every script must include one real citation the viewer can check, one small true narrator admission, and one very specific instruction ("tonight, before the light goes off…").

**CTA and ending**
19. **ONE line** of CTA, at minute 6–12: "If this is helping you, like the video and leave a comment so the algorithm can carry it to someone who needs it." No subscribe plea, no repeat.
20. Ending (last 90 s): restate tonight's single task, give the exact words to say or write, then a short calm line. No "thanks for watching". Write it so the viewer can loop it.

**Anti-copy (most important)**
21. The hook, CTA wording, ending and section labels must NOT repeat anything from Ep.03–12. The QC runs a 6-gram scan against all 12 scripts.
22. Title states the viewer's benefit or action. No author name, no year, no "book".

## 3. HILL FRAME: rules for "we learned this from Hill's life" (owner 2026-11-04)

**How to say it**
H1. Use 3–5 "Hill moments" per script, roughly one per 2–3 sections, as anchors. Each moment = **year + place/occasion + what he did or wrote**, then "what we learned from it" + your instruction.
   Sample lines: "In nineteen twenty-eight, in his first big book, Hill wrote…" / "Hill's point, in plain words, is this…" / "Look at what he did after that…"
H2. Always third person: "Hill", "he". **NEVER** "I, Napoleon Hill…" or speaking in his voice. (The YPP rejection reason was exactly this.)
H3. Hill quotes: 1–3 per script, **verbatim** from his 1928 *The Law of Success* (public domain; archive.org `lawofsuccess0000napo_d7w3`). Every quote goes into `quotes.txt` with the page number. Write the rest as paraphrase, labelled "his point, in plain words…"; paraphrase never goes in quote marks. *Think and Grow Rich* (1937): short quotes OK, long passages no.

**Facts: allowed vs forbidden (source = `02_hill_records/dossier.md`)**
H4. **Say as fact, ONLY** what the dossier marks DOCUMENTED. Examples: born eighteen eighty-three in a one-room cabin in Wise County, Virginia; mother died when he was nine; reporting from his teens; several businesses failed and he started over again (1908 Mobile lumber company, 1915 candy shop, 1930 Catskills estate foreclosure); *The Law of Success* published 1928; at about 53, *Think and Grow Rich* (1937) was typed on a three-hundred-dollar loan from his son Blair; Blair was born without ears, and Hill's own account of teaching him to hear is in TGR chapter two (that is Hill's account, so say "Hill wrote that…").
H5. Anything marked "HILL'S CLAIM ONLY" or CONTESTED must be framed as **"Hill said / Hill wrote that…"**, never as fact.
H6. **FORBIDDEN as fact:** "Hill met Carnegie in 1908", "Carnegie gave him a 20-year mission", "he interviewed 500 millionaires", "he advised Wilson/FDR", "he wrote the FDR speech", "Edison was his friend". The records don't support these (Ep.02 is proof). If you must mention one: "Hill told the story that…"
H7. No new Hill anecdote that isn't in the dossier or in his own 1928/1937 text. If it isn't in the dossier, it doesn't go in. Give every Hill moment one line in `dossier.md` (source + tag).

**Title**
H8. Under the 2026-10-14 override, the title still has no author name. Also, "Napoleon Hill®" and "Think and Grow Rich®" are Foundation trademarks, so don't use them as a brand or series name. *(If the owner wants Hill's name in the title, that is the owner's call.)*

## 4. YOUTUBE POLICY CHECKLIST (YPP): every point must PASS before VO
Basis: YPP "inauthentic content" (2025-07-15; mass-produced or repetitive, superficial differences, slideshows with the same narration), the July-2026 clarification (template/repetitive, emotionally manipulative formulas, interchangeable videos, AI personas giving health/legal/finance advice), "altered or synthetic content" disclosure, misleading metadata, impersonation, reused content.

**Not interchangeable (inauthentic)**
Y1. The hook, CTA, ending, section labels and structure differ from Ep.03–12. 6-gram scan = 0 shared.
Y2. Each script has 3 things a template can't produce: a real citation (Hill 1928 page), a small true narrator admission, and a very specific instruction.
Y3. Don't use the same "Hill moment" in more than one episode (keep each episode's anchor separate).

**Not manipulative**
Y4. No threats ("your family will suffer", "you'll die poor"). No guaranteed results ("you will be rich", "the one percent"). No fake urgency ("watch before it's deleted", "tell no one").
Y5. No health, medical, legal or money advice. The narrator is not presented as a doctor, therapist or adviser.

**Honesty / misinformation**
Y6. Hill's unproven stories are never facts (see section 3, H4–H7). No invented quotes, numbers or dialogue.
Y7. Title, thumbnail and description promise only what the script delivers. No clickbait mismatch.

**Impersonation / AI persona**
Y8. The narrator is NEVER Hill. Don't clone or imitate Hill's voice. Our own narrator voice is fine.
Y9. In the description, write one honest line: "Narrated lesson inspired by Napoleon Hill's public-domain writings. Not affiliated with the Napoleon Hill Foundation." Don't use Hill's name as the channel or brand name.

**AI disclosure**
Y10. If images or video show Hill (or any real person) realistically, in a scene that never happened, turn on **"Altered or synthetic content" = Yes** in YouTube Studio. Better: for Hill himself, use real public-domain photos or a clearly illustrated/painted style.
Y11. A narration voice that isn't anyone's imitation generally doesn't need the label, but the safe practice is: if in doubt, turn on disclosure.

**Reused content / copyright**
Y12. No long readings. At most 1–3 short quotes; the rest is original commentary and instruction.
Y13. Quote only public-domain texts (1928 Law of Success; Conwell, Black, Hunter). Music/SFX licensed only (Mixkit etc.).

## 5. Per-topic briefs

### 5.1 Topic 1: Opportunity is where you are standing
- **Hill anchor (check in the text):** *Law of Success* (1928), the "Initiative and Leadership" lesson; TGR (1937) chapter one, the R. U. Darby "three feet from gold" story (Hill's text, so say "Hill told the story of…").
- **Hill life moment (DOCUMENTED):** every time a business collapsed (1908, 1915, 1930), he started again with what he had: pen, paper, readers. "He didn't look for a new country; he picked up what was already in his hands."
- **Optional support:** one line from Conwell, *Acres of Diamonds* (Gutenberg #368).
- **Spine:** your chance is under your feet: people, skills, and problems you already have. Teach the viewer to look in their own backyard.
- **Open loop:** one question to ask about your own job, street or skills tonight. Reveal it at ~60%.
- **Avoid:** Ep.06 (small job / "nobody is checking"), Ep.08 (age, not too late). Not a money video. Don't make "three feet from gold" a "never quit" adversity lecture (excluded cluster); the angle is **seeing**.
- **Title:** *The Opportunity You Keep Walking Past*

### 5.2 Topic 2: Real friendship
- **Hill anchor (check in the text):** *Law of Success* (1928), "Pleasing Personality" and "Co-operation" lessons. Do NOT make the episode about "Master Mind" (that idea is reserved in the channel's planned list).
- **Hill life moment (DOCUMENTED):** the 1937 book got typed because his son Blair lent three hundred dollars. When everything else had failed, one person close to him put money on the table. "Who is your Blair? Whose Blair are you?"
- **Optional support:** one line from Hugh Black, *Friendship* (1898).
- **Spine:** hundreds of contacts, but who would you call at 3 a.m.? Show up, give time, be honest, go first. Something to do this week.
- **Open loop:** "one message you'll send tonight". Give the exact wording at ~60%.
- **Avoid:** Ep.12 (group chat, friends as voters/scouts), Ep.07 (keep your word). Not a sermon. Don't invent Hill's personal friendships (the Carnegie/Edison "friendships" are forbidden, see H6).
- **Title:** *Who Would You Call at 3 A.M.?*

### 5.3 Topic 3: Energy / pep is back
- **Hill anchor (check in the text):** *Law of Success* (1928), the "Enthusiasm" lesson. This is the main source for 1–2 quotes.
- **Hill life moment (DOCUMENTED):** writing from his teens; after several collapses, a big book at about forty-five (1928) and another at about fifty-three (1937). Hill himself claimed (biography) that by 1929 his royalties were about $2,500/month; that is a CLAIM, so say "it is said that…" or leave it out.
- **Optional support:** one line from W. C. Hunter, *Pep* (1914).
- **Spine:** you're not lazy, you've gone flat. Small daily actions: posture, voice, first task, how you greet people.
- **Open loop:** "the first five minutes rule". Reveal at ~60%.
- **Avoid:** Ep.09 (relax/unclamp), Ep.10 (optimism/hope), morning routine (excluded cluster), health/medical advice. Keep the age angle light, because Ep.08 already did "not too late".
- **Title:** *You're Not Lazy. You've Gone Flat.*
