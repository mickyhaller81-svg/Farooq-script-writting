# CASE SHEET — 05 · Anna-Jane Cheney / Henry Keogh / Dr Colin Manock

Status: RESEARCH v1 (2026-10-02). Web search only. Case 04 (Jubillar) deferred by the owner.

**Legal-risk flag (highest of the slate so far):** Henry Keogh is living. His conviction was quashed and the DPP entered a nolle prosequi. He was **not** declared innocent. In 2018 SA Police said he remains a person of interest, and Anna-Jane's family believes he is guilty and asked for a retrial. Dr Manock may be living. The topic file's line "Keogh is exonerated — never imply guilt" is **corrected**: the script must imply neither guilt nor innocence. It reports what courts and officials said, attributed, and gives both families' positions.

```
CASE:        Death of Anna-Jane Cheney, 29 — Magill (north-eastern Adelaide), South Australia — March 18, 1994
CENTRAL Q:   If the forensic evidence that convicted Henry Keogh was wrong, what does anyone actually know
             about what happened in that bathroom?
ANGLE:       "Two truths": the science that convicted him collapsed, and the suspicion didn't. A system story
             about one expert, told without picking a side the courts never picked.
STRUCTURE:   Reverse chronology (§4 alt, wrongful-conviction stories): open at release, December 2014, and work back.
THE TURN:    (~23:00) The "grip marks" that convicted him were seen by one man, whose own qualifications
             were later called into question, and the 2014 court finds the evidence flawed.
             Counter-turn (~43:00): no retrial, compensation, and police say he is still a person of interest.
```

## VICTIM
**Anna-Jane Cheney**, 29 (turned 29 three days before she died). Head of Professional Conduct at the Law Society of South Australia. Daughter of Dr Kevin Cheney, a haematology pathologist. "Well liked." Her wedding was five weeks away. [SRC 1, 2, 3]
