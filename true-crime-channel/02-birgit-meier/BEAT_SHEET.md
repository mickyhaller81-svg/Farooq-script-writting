# BEAT SHEET — 02 · Birgit Meier

- **Title:** The Police Chief Who Solved His Own Sister's Murder (52 chars)
- **Alt:** The Handcuffs Nobody Threw Away · Her Body Was Under His Garage for 28 Years
- **Structure:** **In medias res** (§4 default). Open at the 2017 dig, rewind to 1989. Video 01 used dual timeline, so the structure differs.
- **Withheld name:** Wichmann is "the gardener" until the 1993 search at the midpoint.
- **Target:** ~7,300 words (±10%).

## 1 Cold open (0:00–1:00)
September 2017. A garage on the edge of Lüneburg, and a jackhammer through the floor of an inspection pit. A man in his seventies stands at the door. He spent his whole career finding other people's missing. **OPEN [SPINE]:** why is the head of a criminal police force digging for his own sister, 28 years late?

## 2 Promise (1:00–1:30)
This isn't a story about new science. It's about one object that survived an order to destroy everything. **OPEN:** what object.

## 3 World before (1:30–5:00)
Birgit as a photographer and mother, her daughter Yasmin, the separation, and the big brother in Hamburg. Plant: the neighbours' gardener; a party where she once met him. **OPEN:** she was due to meet her husband that night and wasn't there.

## 5 The day (5:30–13:30)
August 13–14, 1989: the empty house, Yasmin's report, local police and the two easy theories (suicide; the husband). Wolfgang's instinct says crime. Cost of time. Context: the same summer, two couples were killed in the Göhrde forest, under an hour away (dramatic irony). **OPEN:** someone in Lüneburg had an alibi that didn't add up.

## 6 First response (13:30–22:30)
The husband: the wrong road (7) and his clearance. The gardener: met her at a party, worked for the neighbours, a record. His alibi was an evening with his wife, then walking the dog. He hid that he was on sick leave, and nobody asked (contradiction 3). Wolfgang pushes; his colleagues don't. Comment trigger #1 (inter-force politics: should the brother have been allowed more say?). Years pass. **OPEN:** 1993, a new prosecutor.

## 7 Midpoint turn (22:30–26:00)
The search warrant, and the phone call that warned him. NAME: Kurt-Werner Wichmann. The house: padded soundproof door, a new basement wall, cadaver-dog alerts, handcuffs, sedatives, weapons, a red sports car buried in the garden. He flees and is arrested near Heilbronn. Ten days later he is dead in his cell. **OPEN:** what happens to a case when the suspect dies?

## 8 Complications (26:00–37:00)
Halfway reset. German law means no proceedings against the dead: the file is closed and the evidence ordered destroyed. Birgit is still missing. Wolfgang retires; in 2002/03 he opens his own file and builds a private team (Chedor, Püschel, Strate, Köhnke). In 2003 he learns the evidence is gone. Editorial: the institutional cost of a closed file. Investigator's doubt: the 1993 photos of the house. **OPEN:** something in a basement in Hanover.

## 9 The break (37:00–43:00)
Autumn 2016: handcuffs at the Hanover forensic institute, overlooked in the destruction. A drop of blood, and modern DNA: Birgit. Police reopen with full force. The team studies the former property. The garage's inspection pit is only about 90 cm deep, shallower than it should be. Late September 2017: the concrete comes up and Birgit is found (one clause, no detail). **OPEN:** what else did this man do?

## 10 Resolution (43:00–49:00)
January 2018 autopsy: shot. December 2017: police name Wichmann prime suspect in the Göhrde murders, after skin-cell DNA turns up on 1989 tape lifts from a victim's car. Name the four victims once. Police president: a serial killer who may have killed beyond Germany. No trial is possible. The defence's side, fairly: he can never answer, and the Göhrde cases remain officially unsolved. **OPEN:** the second person.

## 11 Aftermath (49:00–53:00)
The accomplice theory and the open task force. Back to Birgit and Yasmin. Wolfgang: a career of solving other people's cases. Thematic answer: the system didn't fail for lack of a suspect. It failed in the follow-through, and one forgotten object undid the failure. Comment trigger #2: should evidence ever be destroyed in an unsolved case?
