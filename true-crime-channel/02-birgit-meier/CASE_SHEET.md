# CASE SHEET — 02 · Birgit Meier / Wolfgang Sielaff (Göhrde murders)

Status: RESEARCH v1 (2026-10-02). Owner approved autonomous production. Research was done via web search only (direct site fetch blocked in this environment). German-language press used where English is thin.

```
CASE:            Disappearance and murder of Birgit Meier (née Sielaff) — Lüneburg, Lower Saxony, Germany
                 Disappeared night of August 13–14, 1989 · remains found late September 2017
                 Linked: Göhrde murders (two couples, Göhrde State Forest, summer 1989)

CENTRAL Q:       How did a woman whose own brother ran a big-city criminal police force stay missing for
                 28 years — when the answer was a few kilometres from her home the whole time?

ANGLE (§10.1):   The case wasn't solved by new science alone. It was solved by one object that survived
                 an order to destroy everything: a pair of handcuffs nobody remembered to throw away.

THE TURN:        (~23:00) The 1993 search of the gardener's house: a soundproofed room, a freshly built
                 basement wall, handcuffs, a buried car. He had been tipped off by phone and fled. Ten days
                 after his arrest he was dead in his cell, and the case was closed and the evidence ordered
                 destroyed.
                 Second turn (~38:00): the one item that escaped destruction carries Birgit's blood.
```

## VICTIM
**Birgit Meier**, née Sielaff, born July 9, 1948 [SRC 6]. She was 41 when she disappeared (some English coverage says 40).
- Photographer, living in Lüneburg. [SRC 1, 6]
- Mother of a daughter, Yasmin, who filed the missing-person report. [SRC 6, 7]
- Separating from her husband Harald, a businessman; that evening he was due to discuss the separation. [SRC 6]
- Younger sister of Wolfgang Sielaff, then a senior commander of the Hamburg criminal police (described in coverage as head of Hamburg's criminal investigation department / deputy police president [verify exact title]). [SRC 1, 2]
- Thin on personal detail in English sources. Use the photographer's eye and her daughter as the human spine; no invention.

## EVIDENCE SPINE
1. The 1989 interview: weak alibi, concealed sick leave.
2. The 1993 search: soundproofed room, handcuffs, buried car, cadaver-dog alerts, then the destruction order.
3. The surviving handcuffs → Birgit's blood → the garage pit.

## UNKNOWNS
- Who tipped Wichmann off, and why.
- Whether he had an accomplice.
- How many people he killed.
- Exact date of the 2017 find (27th vs 29th).
- Exactly how Birgit and Wichmann's paths crossed on August 13–14.
