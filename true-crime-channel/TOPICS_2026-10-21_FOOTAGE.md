# FOOTAGE-FIRST TOPIC SHORTLIST — 2026-10-21

Three cases picked because the body cam, interrogation, surveillance and courtroom video are **already public** (police-posted or court-streamed), plus photos. Companion to `FOOTAGE_SOURCES.md` and `TOPICS_2026-10-02.json`.

Licence key for each footage item:
- **GOV** = produced and released by a police department, prosecutor or court. Use in full (see `FOOTAGE_SOURCES.md` §4).
- **NEWS** = TV station / Court TV / network. Copyrighted. Short clip (5–10 s) under narration, on-screen credit, expect Content ID claim.
- **[verify]** = confirm against a tier 1–3 source before narration (rules §2.2).

All three are US, legally resolved, adult perpetrators, adult victims, no family objection found. Saturation checked 2026-10-21.

## F1 — Dalia Dippolito / Michael Dippolito — Boynton Beach, Florida — 2009 → 2017
**Tier A · rating 9.1 · saturation MOD** (20/20 "Down Payment on Death" 2020, Court TV "Legendary Trials" 2023, Casefile ep. 145; no 50-min YouTube treatment found)

- **Central question:** Was a newlywed caught hiring a hitman on camera, or did a police department with a reality-TV crew build the crime it filmed?
- **The turn (midpoint):** Three trials. Convicted 2011 (20 years), overturned on appeal 2014, hung jury 2016, convicted again 2017 (16 years) [verify sentence]. The defence that nearly worked: the sting was entertainment first, police work second.
- **Victim:** Michael Dippolito, the husband, alive. He spoke on record repeatedly; he is the human centre. Rules §13: never call the police operation "staged" in a way that implies the victim faked anything; the police did stage the scene, and that is the documented fact.
- **Footage inventory**
  - GOV: Boynton Beach PD YouTube: the behind-the-scenes "staged crime scene" video (posted by the department the same day, Aug 5, 2009), Dalia's reaction at the fake scene, the undercover meeting with the officer posing as the hitman ("5,000 percent sure" line [verify exact wording]), the interrogation-room video where the detective brings the undercover officer in.
  - GOV: Palm Beach County court records, three trials. Florida cameras-in-court; WPTV live-streamed all three (NEWS for their feed; court's own record is public).
  - NEWS: COPS episode footage (Langley Productions, copyrighted; clip only), 20/20 interviews.
  - Photos: booking photo (GOV), court stills (NEWS), the house exterior (shoot your own / Street View).
- **Structure suggestion:** Reverse-trial spine (start at the 2017 verdict, work back to the tape). Different from every structure used in 01–10.
- **Legal note:** Dippolito convicted; husband living and named; undercover officer named in court record. No minors.

## F2 — Samantha Josephson / Nathaniel Rowland — Columbia, South Carolina — 2019 → 2021
**Tier B · rating 9.0 · saturation MOD** (local TV, a few mid-size channels, no major network doc found; "Sami's Law" coverage is policy news, not true crime)

- **Central question:** A college senior gets into the wrong car at 2 a.m. How did police find the driver in under 24 hours, and what was inside the car that convicted him?
- **The turn (midpoint):** The arrest. An officer sees a black Impala the night after, the driver runs, and the car holds her phone, her blood, bleach wipes, and child-safety locks that would have made the rear doors unopenable [verify each item against trial record].
- **Victim:** Samantha Josephson, 21, University of South Carolina senior from Robbinsville, New Jersey, days from graduation, heading to law school at Drexel [verify]. Her father Seymour Josephson speaks for her (Sami's Law advocacy; family welcomed coverage that pushes rideshare safety).
- **Footage inventory**
  - GOV: Columbia PD surveillance release of Samantha getting into the Impala in Five Points (released by the department in 2019).
  - GOV: Columbia PD body cam of the traffic stop and foot chase (played at trial; SC public record once admitted).
  - GOV: Full trial July 2021, Richland County, Judge Clifton Newman; South Carolina allows cameras. Streamed by WIS-TV and Law&Crime (NEWS for their feed) — request the court's own recording or use the clerk's exhibit copies.
  - GOV: Sentencing July 27, 2021, life without parole, including Seymour Josephson's statement.
  - NEWS: Family interviews (local TV, NBC), clip only.
  - Photos: evidence photos admitted at trial (public exhibits), Five Points street (own footage / Street View), Sami's Law signing (government photo).
- **Structure suggestion:** 24-hour clock. The whole first half runs on a timeline from 1:30 a.m. to the arrest the next evening.
- **Legal note:** Never narrate the wound count. Rowland maintained innocence at sentencing; say "convicted", quote the verdict.

## F3 — Lauren Giddings / Stephen McDaniel — Macon, Georgia — 2011 → 2014
**Tier A · rating 8.8 · saturation MOD-HIGH** (Dateline "The Watcher"; the live-TV interview clip is widely reposted; several mid-size YouTube channels. Still no 50-min victim-centred treatment found, which is the angle.)

- **Central question:** When a reporter told the "worried neighbour" live on air that remains had been found, why did his face change, and what had police already found in his apartment?
- **The turn (midpoint):** The WGXA interview of June 30, 2011. The viewer sees it early as a strange moment; at the midpoint they learn McDaniel was a Mercer Law classmate who had been watching her for months [verify "months"] and that the hacksaw packaging was already in the building's trash.
- **Victim:** Lauren Giddings, 27, Mercer University law graduate, studying for the bar, from Maryland. Family (parents, sister Kaitlyn) have spoken publicly and supported the Dateline treatment [verify current stance].
- **Footage inventory**
  - GOV: Macon Police Department posted McDaniel's full first interrogation on YouTube (June 11, 2015). This is the spine.
  - GOV: Bibb County Superior Court hearings and the April 2014 guilty plea (Georgia allows cameras; local stations recorded; court record public).
  - NEWS: WGXA live interview (copyrighted by the station; the key 10 seconds are fair-use commentary territory with credit).
  - NEWS: Dateline interviews with family and investigators, clip only.
  - Photos: apartment complex exterior (own / Street View), Mercer campus (own), booking photo (GOV), evidence photos from the plea hearing (GOV).
- **Structure suggestion:** Two cameras. Alternate the public camera (the TV interview) with the private camera (the interrogation room). Different from 03's single interrogation spine.
- **Legal note:** Guilty plea 2014, life with parole eligibility after 30 years [verify]. No graphic description of the dismemberment beyond what the viewer needs (§0.4); say "remains".
